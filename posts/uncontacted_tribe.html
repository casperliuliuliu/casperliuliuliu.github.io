Uncontacted Tribe

Published: Febuary 03, 2026

Listening to the podcast of Lex Fridman Podcast interviewing Paul, a jungle keeper who has directly interacted with uncontacted tribes, completely pulled me in. The stories are fascinating. Seeing the footage of those encounters, it's almost unbelievable how fit and muscular these people are, and the long-haired warriors who look both ancient and strikingly cool. What really stayed with me was the mixed feeling Paul described, fear and excitement intertwined. Hearing about the incidents he and his friends experienced made me realize how fragile human life is. And yet, amid all that danger, there was this one scene that felt almost magical, the tension between the research group and the uncontacted tribe slowly easing because of bananas and plantains. The man named Ignacio, who had nearly been killed by the tribe, was able to wave at them. They waved back. They even danced a little, back and forth. Even at its most dangerous edge like that, life is still meant to be enjoyed. Paul also talked about how he entered jungle conservation and research. He doesn't have a PhD. He simply spent decades in the jungle, learning directly from local people. That hit me hard. It reminded me of my own passion for becoming an AI researcher. A PhD is still a reasonable and meaningful path toward the future I imagine, but I realized I've been unconsciously limiting myself. I've been telling myself that “real” research can only begin once I'm finally inside the environment I dream of. But that's not true. You can start now. As long as you put in the effort, nothing is impossible. All of this also reminded me of when my girlfriend and I watched Zootopia 2 this Sunday. One line from the snake really struck me: “We shall succeed.” After nearly a hundred years of effort, hardship, and danger, the burden wasn't framed as something crushing, it was carried with a faith that simple. Maybe that's how we should face our own lives too. With effort, yes. But also with ease, optimism, and the quiet confidence to say: we shall succeed.
