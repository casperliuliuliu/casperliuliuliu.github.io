How to Burn Out in 1 Day

Published: March 15, 2026

Burnout is a feeling of being so mentally and physically exhausted that you cannot work effectively.

Yesterday I was preparing a PowerPoint for my PI. I wanted it to be detailed but also simple enough for people to understand easily. This took a lot of time and effort, so I ended up working until 4 a.m.

I actually enjoy making clear and engaging presentations, but doing this late at night feels like torture. What makes it worse is that today I woke up late and felt terrible. I could not focus during the day, and the whole day felt unproductive. Then I started feeling like I lacked discipline.

This cycle gets worse when I still try to work even though my body actually needs rest.

Solution

If I do stay up late, I should actually get the minimum sleep I need and try to wake up at roughly the same time as usual to prevent my biological rhythm from shifting too much. But nap if needed.

I should also accept the fact that the next day will likely be less productive. Instead of forcing myself to work at full capacity, the focus should be on recovery and returning to a normal schedule with low-cognitive-demand work.

Most importantly, sleep should always have the highest priority. When possible, I should stop working earlier and continue the task the next day rather than pushing through exhaustion, or simply plan ahead and finish the work well before the deadline.

This reminds me of a story. A friend of mine once talked to a professor that he regretted pursuing a PhD because he hated writing papers late at night. The professor replied, “I also hate writing papers late at night. But when I write papers in the morning or afternoon, it doesn't feel that bad." "I believe no one really enjoys writing papers late at night.”

Yes, working late at night simply makes everything harder and slowly burns away the passion and joy in the work itself.
