Still research as a employee

Published: March 16, 2026

Recently, I have been looking for labs to join for my U of T research exchange, and I suddenly realized that I was falling into the same pattern as before. I was still thinking about joining labs that publish the most papers, have strong connections with major companies like Google or Amazon, and could give me more experience for my PhD applications.

This realization hit me hard. In many ways, this mindset is not very different from looking for a high-paying job. It is difficult to escape the influence of society's evaluation system. I noticed that I was again considering working on problems that are easier to publish rather than the problems I truly want to solve.

But if I eventually enter a PhD program and still cannot work on the problems I genuinely care about, if I keep choosing research topics mainly for better opportunities, then when will I actually start pursuing the deeper problems I want to explore, like technology acceleration.

I am grateful that I became aware of this early. Otherwise, I might continue living under the shadow of pursuing fashionable research topics and following others' directions for the rest of my career.

Yes, aligning research topics with popular directions is still important. However, what matters most is having a clear plan and a genuine reason for working on a particular research problem. So, for my future PhD path, I should first identify the research problems that truly matter to me, and then connect them with relevant research directions and labs that can support that pursuit.
